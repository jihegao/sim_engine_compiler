# E13.var01: traditional subitem robbing, matched control

This bounded case extends the accepted [E03 two-aircraft shared-spare parent](../E03-two-aircraft-shared-spare-v1.0.0/README.md). Two same-type aircraft belong to one unit at BASE-01 and fly paired two-hour missions from h8, with seed 1 and a 36-hour horizon. Each aircraft carries traditional critical LRU-A and LRU-B. Both local opening stocks are zero. A's failure rate is 1,800,000 and B's is 4,200,000 per million operating hours; their sum equals System.FRT of 6,000,000. A's direct repair lasts 12 hours and B's four. Item and Station ENROB overrides remain null. The installed SIMLOX manual physical pp223, 232 and 255, plus the installed database dictionary, govern this setup. The E13 mechanism is limited to traditional critical subitem replacement.

Four immutable diagnostic packages document the source progression: v0.1.0 produced A/A failures, v0.1.1 produced A/B failures with the first A repair too short to make a suitable donor, v0.1.2 produced the enabled `ENROB=Y` robbing action, and v0.1.3 is its matched `ENROB=N` control. Only `Control.ENROB` and `ProblemDescription.PDESC` differ between the last two authored inputs. Their pre-run contracts, exact parent diffs, inputs, builders, guarded session-0 SSH scripts and diagnostic decisions are in the sibling packages. The two positive/control raw runs each have 12 files sealed by their `closure.json`, retained under ignored local `runs/`.

In both arms, aircraft 0 fails physical A0 at h8.09414 and aircraft 1 fails B1 at h8.15663. Both missions abort. A repair starts h8.35496 and B repair h8.47992. With robbing enabled, the aircraft 1 repair task starts to rob at h8.47992. Robbing task 4 removes healthy physical B2 from aircraft 0, which is already down waiting for A. Aircraft 1 receives B2 at h8.64659 and is ready at h8.97992. The repaired B1 returns to aircraft 0 at h12.4799; that aircraft remains down for A and is ready at h20.6883. With robbing disabled, aircraft 1 waits for its own repaired B1 and becomes ready at h12.8133. Aircraft 0 is ready at h20.6883 in both arms. The recipient's observed return to service is 3.83338 hours earlier without delaying the donor in this run.

The native converter reports zero errors and warning #108 for simulation logging in each arm. Both import/export roundtrips preserve all 20 populated authored tables. Both reports contain 72 half-hour SystemResults and StockTotalResults rows. ResultSummary and all StockTotalResults rows are identical across arms; SystemResults differs in 42 cells over bins 16–25, including the native NROBT count. The report summary records the same two aborted missions in both arms; this case does not claim a mission success gain. Explicit maintenance tasks request no resources, and no resource table is populated. Both arms end with two ready aircraft and no open backorders.

Run `validate_pair.py` for the author read-only replay and `review/independent_replay.py` for the separate replay. The signoff and evidence index record their verdicts and source/raw seals. This one-seed comparison establishes the observed SIMLOX traditional robbing path and timing effect under these conditions. It does not establish other E13 variants, explicit failure-mode/CM-task robbing, resource scarcity, stochastic frequency, GUI behavior or cross-engine equivalence. Issue #18 remains open.
