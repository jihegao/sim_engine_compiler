## Observed behavior and ledgers

`crew-before` and `crew-equal` both request crew for aircraft 0 and 1 at 07:30. Aircraft 0 leases and works through 08:00; aircraft 1 waits, receives the released crew, and works through 08:30. Both crew leases are released before the single two-aircraft launch at 08:30, followed by success and completion at 09:30. The 0.51-hour setting puts the declared cutoff at 08:30:36; the 0.50-hour setting puts it at 08:30 exactly. The equal-time log orders second preparation completion and crew release before launch, establishing that observation for this fixed model only.

`crew-after` sets the declared cutoff to 08:29:24. The first aircraft finishes at 08:00; the second starts then and remains in work. At 08:29:24, the mission cancels, the second subtask is ordered to stop, its partially completed preparation ends, proportional turnround factor 0.98 is recorded, the crew is freed and released, and both aircraft return ready. In the [08:00,08:30) aggregate, `ResourceResults.NRUM` is approximately 0.98, `SystemResults.NSYMP` 0.98 and `NSYRY` 0.04; these are time-weighted interval quantities, not a third aircraft.

`aircraft-missing` has `SystemDeployment.QTYPS=1` with two crew units. One aircraft is allocated and prepares from 07:30 to 08:00, with one crew lease and release. The mission logs a wait with timeout at 08:00, then cancels at 08:00 without a second aircraft event. `crew-zero` retains two aircraft but has `ResourceAllocation.RQTY=0`; both distinct owners request and wait, no crew leases or work occur, then the mission cancels at 08:00 and both waiters stop. `mission cancelled:` itself has no reason code, so each shortage conclusion is limited to its source row and trace/ledger differential.

Each final report contains 72 contiguous half-hour `SystemResults` and `ResourceResults` intervals over 0–36 hours. The independent replay checked every row against fixed occupancy, waiting, ready/assigned stocks, preparation, launch and result summary. All runs end with zero assigned or preparing aircraft, zero active crew leases and zero resource waiters; the final `NSYRY` is two except the one-aircraft model, where it is one. Successful runs show one completed wave and two sorties; cancelled runs show none.

## Nonpublished probe and remaining limits

The `horizon-wait` discovery run `c04-v100-horizon-wait-ssh-20260929-01` is preserved in its own local directory. SIMLOX rejected `ResultCollection.RCEND=Control.SIMPE=8` with error 107 (collection end must be less than simulation period), and rejected the operation with error 204 (some missions may not return before the simulation period). It exited calculation without a valid SXO; `run.log` SHA-256 is `cd069066f069b8e5141a7692e26fd6a27a112fe623453cf753fae90fa3d4c282`. It is not an accepted run. The smallest future experiment is a profile whose latest possible mission return fits within SIMPE while a resource wait remains active at the result horizon; C04 does not infer that state here.

The aircraft-shortage case proves persistent one-aircraft insufficiency, not a second aircraft returning just before/at/after the cutoff. Only crew readiness is swept around the cutoff. A separate scheduled aircraft-release model would be needed for the aircraft-return boundary. No universal cancellation reason, same-time priority, nonzero proportional turnround duration, GUI-only behavior, cross-engine parity or performance conclusion follows from these fixed CLI cases.
